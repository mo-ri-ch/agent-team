Your team of bots

A bot is a profile

In Hermes, each bot is a profile: its own folder, with its own settings, keys, instructions, memory and chat. That's the role you met at the start, made real: SOUL.md holds its instructions, and config.yaml its model and tools.
bot = SOUL.md (role) + config.yaml (model, tools)

Marketing's folder. Tap a file to see what it holds.

Because each bot has its own folder, Marketing's memory and keys never mix with Production's.

Let's hire the first one.

Hire your first bot

In the Hermes Desktop app, Bot Mode is on by default: open Bots, choose New Agent, and give it a name, a title and a one-line description. Advanced lets you pick its model, write its SOUL.md and choose its skills and tools. In the terminal, one command makes the profile, and the bot's name becomes a command of its own.
hermes profile create marketing → marketing chat

Pick how you'll do it.
  1. Open Hermes Desktop → Bots → New Agent.
  2. Name: Marketing · Title: Marketing lead
  3. Description: Plans posts and captions for the social media manager.
  4. Advanced → pin a model, and paste its SOUL.md (next page).
  5. Save. Marketing appears in your roster, with its own chat.

Make the profile. It also makes the command marketing.

hermes profile create marketing

Pick its model (the same questions as hermes model):

marketing setup

Write its instructions, then chat:

nano ~/.hermes/profiles/marketing/SOUL.md
marketing chat

Checked against Hermes Agent v0.23 (November 2026). Bot Mode is new and changes often: if a step looks different, see the Bot Mode docs.

🤔 What is nano?
A simple text editor inside the terminal. Type or paste, then press Ctrl+O and Enter to save, and Ctrl+X to leave. Any text editor works too: the file is just text.

Its SOUL.md decides how it works. What goes in it?

Write a good SOUL.md

SOUL.md is read before every chat, like the instructions you wrote by hand. A good one is short and covers four things: who the bot helps, its job, what it must never do, and who it hands work to.
SOUL.md: who you help · your job · never · hand off to

Pick a bot. Tick the parts. Watch its SOUL.md.

The “never” lines matter most: they're the bot's half of the safety story.

🤔 Will it always obey its “never” lines?
No. SOUL.md guides the model, but a model can slip. Real limits come from the program: which tools a bot has (later in this lesson), its own sandbox (Lesson 8) and approvals (Lesson 11).

A model for each bot

Each bot can use a different model. Routine jobs, like packing lists, run fine on a cheap one. Writing posts, or reading weekly numbers, may need a stronger one. You pay for each bot's own tokens at its model's price.
team cost = sum of (bot's tokens × its price)
Operations: 3 million tokens × $0.10 = $0.30. Marketing: 2 million × $1.00 = $2. Together: $2.30 a month.

Pick a model for each bot. Watch the monthly cost.

Now put them in one room.

The team chat

A group chat holds 2 to 6 bots. When you @mention bots, those answer, and they can @mention each other to hand work on. After each of your messages, the bots get at most 3 rounds, then it's your turn again: the round limit, built in.
2–6 bots · @mention who answers · 3 rounds max

Tools for each bot

The MCP servers a bot can use are listed in its own config.yaml, under mcp_servers. So each bot gets only the tools its role needs. After you change them, type /reload-mcp in that bot's chat.
a bot's tools live in its own config.yaml

Which bot should get each tool?

# ~/.hermes/profiles/production/config.yaml (an example)
mcp_servers:
  orders:
    url: "https://orders.example.com/mcp"

Make a new bot only when a role needs its own tools, keys, memory or schedule. Four good bots beat ten vague ones.
